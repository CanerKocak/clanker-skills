# Tighten a draft

Try deleting a paragraph or sentence before polishing its words. Read across
the cut. What fact, instruction, or turn in the writer’s voice would the reader
lose? If nothing is lost, make the cut. If one clause supplies a reason or
consequence, save that clause.

Look for openings that announce the next sentence, praise that adds nothing
to the evidence, and endings that repeat the opening. Within a sentence, cut
words that do the same job. Replace a phrase with its buried verb when the
verb says it all.

Test small cuts against the source. Dropping “only” from “Only editors may
publish” leaves open who else may publish. Keep limits, uncertainty,
attribution, and units. Use figures only when the source supplies them.
In dialogue, keep both beats of “Wait. Wait for me.” when the speaker’s
insistence matters.

| Status note | Line edit |
| --- | --- |
| Latency is faster now. Median latency fell from 20 ms to 5 ms. | Median latency fell from 20 ms to 5 ms. |
| We may possibly need to close early. | We may need to close early. |
| The bridge reopened. This is good news because buses can cross again. | The bridge reopened. Buses can cross again. |

Return the edited passage in its original format. Leave effective lines alone.
Explain a cut when the user asks for the reasoning.
